# Sample note 5: infective endocarditis with heart failure and prolonged IV antibiotic therapy

**Patient:** 58-year-old woman
**Admission diagnosis:** Native mitral-valve infective endocarditis complicated by acute heart failure

## Discharge diagnoses
- Mitral-valve infective endocarditis
- Acute on chronic heart failure with reduced EF
- Pulmonary edema, resolved
- Chronic kidney disease
- Anemia of chronic disease
- History of intravenous drug use, in remission

## Hospital course
The patient was admitted with fever, malaise, progressive dyspnea, and lower-extremity edema. Blood cultures repeatedly grew methicillin-susceptible Staphylococcus aureus. Echocardiography demonstrated a mitral-valve vegetation with significant mitral regurgitation. She was initially treated with empiric IV antibiotics and subsequently narrowed to a pathogen-directed IV antibiotic regimen after infectious-disease consultation.

Cardiothoracic surgery evaluated the patient and recommended continued medical therapy with close outpatient follow-up at this time. Her hospitalization was complicated by acute pulmonary edema and decompensated systolic heart failure. She required aggressive IV diuresis with subsequent clinical improvement. Guideline-directed heart-failure therapy was cautiously restarted as blood pressure and renal function permitted.

At discharge, she was afebrile with cleared follow-up blood cultures, improved respiratory status, and stable renal function. She requires completion of a prolonged course of IV antimicrobial therapy through a supervised outpatient program.

## Discharge medications

**New medications**
- Cefazolin IV at the infectious-disease-directed dose and schedule, to complete the planned endocarditis treatment course.
- Furosemide 40 mg by mouth once daily for ongoing volume management.
- Losartan 25 mg once daily, initiated at a low dose because of improving blood pressure and renal function.

**Continue**
- Metoprolol succinate at the discharge dose.
- Other chronic medications after final medication reconciliation.

**Stopped**
- Empiric inpatient antibiotics that were no longer indicated after culture-directed therapy.
- NSAIDs: avoid unless specifically approved.

## Discharge instructions
- Complete the entire IV antibiotic course.
- Attend all infectious-disease and cardiology follow-up appointments.
- Obtain laboratory monitoring according to the outpatient antimicrobial-treatment plan.
- Keep vascular-access/PICC care appointments and follow instructions for the line.
- Do not independently stop or change IV antibiotics.
- Monitor daily weight and symptoms of heart failure.
- Follow a low-sodium diet and prescribed fluid plan.
- Follow any activity restrictions or dental/invasive-procedure recommendations provided by the infectious-disease/cardiology teams.

## Return precautions
Seek urgent medical attention for:
- Fever, chills, or recurrent systemic illness.
- New or worsening shortness of breath.
- Chest pain.
- Fainting or severe dizziness.
- New neurologic symptoms such as weakness, facial droop, difficulty speaking, or confusion.
- Rapidly worsening edema or weight gain.
- Bleeding or significant drainage/redness around the vascular-access site.
- Sudden severe back, chest, or abdominal pain.
- Any concern for a complication related to the IV line.

## Nursing discharge teaching / what nursing typically relays
- The exact antibiotic schedule and duration, while emphasizing that the full course must be completed.
- PICC/vascular-access care and infection-prevention practices.
- Which symptoms could indicate recurrent infection or heart failure.
- Daily weights and when to contact the clinical team.
- Medication reconciliation, particularly new heart-failure medications and discontinued inpatient antibiotics.
- Laboratory and infusion appointments.
- Who to contact after discharge for medication/line problems.
- Emergency symptoms requiring immediate evaluation.
- Teach-back: patient demonstrates understanding of antibiotic administration/line-care instructions or confirms the responsible caregiver can perform them correctly.
- Confirmation that home services, infusion supplies, transportation, and follow-up appointments are arranged before discharge.

## Notes for our pipeline (team notes)
- Cefazolin has no dose or schedule in the note: this MUST be flagged, never guessed.
- Stroke-like symptoms and PICC site problems belong in the RED (911) list.
- Sensitive history (IV drug use) must not be repeated in patient-facing text unless the clinician includes it.
